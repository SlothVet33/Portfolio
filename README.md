# CS-250 GitHub Portfolio Reflection

## Sprint Review and Retrospective

This portfolio entry reflects on my work in CS-250 and the SNHU Travel project. Throughout this project, I explored the responsibilities of several Scrum roles, including Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developer, and Tester. The project provided practical experience with Agile planning, user stories, backlog refinement, testing, stakeholder feedback, and continuous improvement.

The SNHU Travel project also demonstrated how Scrum-Agile practices can support a software development team when requirements change. Instead of relying only on detailed requirements defined at the beginning of the project, Agile allowed the team to refine user stories, adjust priorities, and improve the product through feedback.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by focusing on what the user is trying to accomplish, not just on the technical features of the program. In the SNHU Travel project, user needs were gathered through stakeholder feedback and focus group discussions. These needs were then translated into user stories that described the feature from the user’s perspective.

User stories help because they connect development work directly to customer value. A user story explains who the user is, what they want, and why they need it. This format helps the team avoid building features simply because they seem useful from a technical standpoint. Instead, each feature is connected to a real user goal.

For example, user stories in the SNHU Travel project focused on travelers receiving destination recommendations, customizing travel preferences, and viewing top travel destinations. These stories helped guide development and testing because they clearly described the expected user outcome. They also made it easier to create acceptance criteria and test cases, which helped confirm that the completed feature actually met the user’s need.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking larger requirements into smaller, manageable pieces of work. This makes the development process easier to organize, test, and improve. Rather than trying to build an entire system at once, I believe it is more effective to develop software incrementally, gather feedback, and make adjustments throughout the process.

In future development work, I hope to incorporate several Agile processes, including Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, backlog refinement, Sprint Reviews, and Sprint Retrospectives. Sprint Planning helps establish clear goals and priorities before work begins. Daily Scrums help the team stay aligned and identify blockers early. Backlog refinement improves the quality of requirements before development starts. Sprint Reviews provide an opportunity to collect stakeholder feedback, and Sprint Retrospectives allow the team to reflect on what worked well and what should be improved.

The Agile process I found especially valuable was backlog refinement. During the SNHU Travel project, some user stories needed more detail before they could be fully developed or tested. Refining those stories helped clarify expectations and reduce confusion. In future projects, I would place strong emphasis on clear acceptance criteria, early testing input, and continuous communication with stakeholders.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, collaborating with others, staying accountable, and remaining open to feedback. Software development is rarely completed by one person working alone. It requires coordination between Product Owners, Scrum Masters, Developers, Testers, stakeholders, and users.

A good team member contributes to the team’s goals instead of focusing only on individual tasks. This includes asking questions when requirements are unclear, sharing progress honestly, helping identify risks, and supporting continuous improvement. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned that communication is not separate from development. It is a necessary part of building successful software.

As a Tester, I saw the importance of asking clarifying questions when user stories or acceptance criteria were incomplete. This helped improve the quality of both the requirements and the final product. As a Scrum team member, I also learned that collaboration, transparency, and adaptability are essential. A strong team member helps the team deliver customer value while remaining flexible when requirements change.

## Conclusion

The SNHU Travel project helped me understand how Agile and Scrum support successful software development. User stories helped translate customer needs into actionable development work. Scrum events helped organize the project, improve communication, and support continuous feedback. Testing helped verify that completed features met user expectations.

Overall, this project showed me that effective software development requires more than writing code. It requires understanding users, communicating with the team, adapting to change, and continuously improving the product. These are Agile practices and professional habits that I plan to carry forward into future development work.
